## Mission

Aerostratospheric designs, builds, and flies ultra-lightweight high-altitude platforms to collect and publicly release high-quality atmospheric data.

We focus on aerospace-engineered platforms and stratospheric research. Payloads are built in the United States, flown under FAA Part 101 guidelines, and the resulting datasets are released openly and rapidly — typically within 48 hours of successful recovery.

## Flagship System — X2Griffon

**X2Griffon** is our ~**1.01 lb** Midwest-made stratospheric research platform carrying:

- Temperature, humidity & pressure sensors
- GPS-based wind profiling
- Real-time APRS + WSPR telemetry
- Dual-camera stratospheric imaging

Complementary systems include MAD-AP mid-altitude drift platforms and continuous ground observations that contribute to the **NASA GLOBE** program (registration **GO-4VW9B**).

**Next public flight:** September 19, 2026 · Casey, Illinois

## Unified Open Global Weather (UOGW)

**One of the most comprehensive open multi-layer atmospheric commons available.**

UOGW is Aerostratospheric’s versioned, machine-readable atmospheric data commons. It is curated under Midwest Stratospheric Data Systems, updated by independent daily GitHub Actions, and published for unrestricted research and educational reuse.

**Current coverage:**
- **25+ datasets** across **6 layers** — Surface · Marine · Upper-air · Stratosphere · Satellite · Flight
- 2,900+ IGRA stations · 1,900+ NDBC stations · 132,000+ GHCN stations
- Continuous Casey, IL ground observations
- First-party near-space flight products

## Core Principles

- **Open Data** — Rapid public release. Attribution required; commercial use welcome under stated terms.
- **Midwest-made** — Designed, assembled, and critical electronics built in-house in the United States.
- **Safety First** — Insured operations, documented procedures, public telemetry, respect for airspace and property.
- **Lightweight** — Ultra-low mass systems for simpler FAA Part 101 compliance and more frequent flights.
- **Transparent** — Full public telemetry and open-science practices.

## Why This Work Matters

Vertical profiles of temperature, moisture, and wind remain sparse relative to satellite and surface networks. High-quality balloon data supports forecasting, severe-weather research, atmospheric process studies, education, and open science.

Data is released for researchers, meteorologists, STEM educators, the amateur radio & HAB community, and citizen scientists.
